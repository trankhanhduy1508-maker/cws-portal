# CWS UE5 end-to-end benchmark correction — 2026-08-22

## Critical correction

The earlier `17.434s UE5 + 5.37s encode` result was not an end-to-end `.blend -> UE5 -> 2K` benchmark. The input was an existing Blender/Cycles beauty plate.

The plate was generated by:

`.cws_tmp/B4_JOB/render_blender_plate_sequence.py`

with Blender/Cycles, 640x360, 4 samples, denoising, frames 432–491. The first saved frame timestamp was `2026-08-21 12:11:11`; the last saved frame timestamp was `2026-08-21 12:31:50`. This is a measured lower bound of `20m39s` for the plate-render stage alone. The exact process-start overhead was not retained, so the benchmark is reported as a lower bound, not an artificially precise total.

Adding the measured downstream stages:

- Blender/Cycles plate stage: at least `20m39s`.
- UE5 SM5 direct-child MRQ: `17.434s`.
- 2K encode: approximately `5.37s`.
- End-to-end lower bound: approximately `21m02s`, before unrecorded Blender startup/scene-load overhead.

Therefore the plate route is useful as a UE5 downstream benchmark, but it is not a valid production claim for a fast `.blend -> UE5` pipeline when beauty plates must be pre-rendered.

## Direct-transfer evidence

The existing Blender For Unreal Engine 4.4.8 route starts from the `.blend` and does not require a pre-rendered beauty plate:

- BFUE export log: collection static export completed in `14.642s`.
- UE scene/import preparation process: approximately `5m22s` (`11:00:57` to `11:06:20`).
- UE MRQ basis-fix process: approximately `3m55s` (`11:06:48` to `11:10:43`).
- Representative output: `.cws_tmp/B4_JOB/BFUE_Gate_Render/BFUE_Representative_Sequence_BasisFix.0000.png`.

The output is visibly far from the Blender reference: camera/composition is wrong, the subject is not preserved correctly, and materials/lighting do not match. The BFUE export log also reports `0 Camera(s)`, so the direct route did not carry a verified shot/camera contract.

Classification: direct transfer is faster than a full Cycles beauty render, but it fails the representative visual gate by a large margin and is not a 90–95% solution.

## Edge-energy verification

At the matched representative frame 0030 / Blender frame 0462, using the same PIL `FIND_EDGES` measurement:

| image | edge mean | RGB MAE | RGB RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Blender reference | 9.0082 | — | — |
| UE5 baseline | 6.6120 | 73.2551 | 79.3891 |
| UE5 no-AA | 15.6393 | 72.7127 | 78.9129 |

The no-AA image is visibly clearer around hair, glasses/eyes, face outline, hand and clothing seams and improves MAE/RMSE slightly. However, its edge mean is materially above the Blender reference; this means part of the increase is likely aliasing/jagged edge energy, not recovered scene detail. It is a useful fast benchmark candidate, not proof of 90–95% visual fidelity.

## Decision

- Keep `CWS_B4_UE5_FastSharp_NoAA_2K.mp4` as a fast downstream benchmark.
- Do not report its 17.434s + 5.37s as true end-to-end `.blend` runtime.
- Reject pre-rendered beauty plates as the production solution for this direct `.blend -> UE5` goal.
- Reject the current BFUE direct-transfer result for quality.
- Do not render full Blender/Cycles native 2K.
- The current evidence-backed bottleneck is semantic transfer: rigged character geometry/animation, Blender materials, camera conventions and lighting/color management. A materially different bounded transfer implementation is required before a true fast `.blend -> UE5` PASS.
